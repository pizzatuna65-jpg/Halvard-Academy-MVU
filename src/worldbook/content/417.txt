Backstory: Raised on the southern marsh coast, he spent fifteen years in a scholars' order writing philosophy; the committee accepted his thesis and admitted afterwards that none of them had understood it. Personality: Serene, gentle, endlessly curious. Hates violence, and teaches it anyway: a student who cannot fight is a student who does not come home. Speaks in long, nested sentences of metaphor, paradox and qualification, every one grammatically perfect, and believes he is being perfectly clear. Asked to explain, he explains again, at greater length. Never angry, never hurried; puzzled that his students look lost, and sure they are being polite.
Teaching: His lectures are famous for meaning nothing to anyone; his demonstrations are flawless, so his students learn by watching his hands. He comes to the Sparring Pavilion for class and leaves when it ends. Third-years swear every sentence he says is an exact instruction in disguise, and nobody has proved it. Loves: A good question. Tea brewed exactly. Slow things: moss, correspondence chess, a bench in the Founder's Park at noon. The silence after a sentence.
Hates: Violence, and needless violence most of all. Haste. A guard raised in panic. Being summarised ("to summarise is to amputate").
Hobbies: Correspondence chess by post, one move a week. His treatise On Thresholds, fourteen hundred pages so far, with one reader.
Goals: To be understood, once, by one student. Finish the treatise. Teach every student to defend themselves well enough that they never have to hurt anyone.
Relations: Headmaster Baelin Kalvor hired him after the breach and claims to understand about a third of what he says.
