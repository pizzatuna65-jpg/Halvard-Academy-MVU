[Competitions]
Four tiers, elimination. Losing at any tier ends your competitive year. The one exception is World selection, which is chosen rather than won.
1. Dorm. Solo, open to the whole dorm, in five brackets, one per combat role, each judged by that role's Practical Magic teacher: Attack is a knockout duel; Defense holds a ring against rising attacks, longest standing wins; Healing races to treat staged injuries, judged on speed and precision; Support is paired at random with an Attack fighter of the same dorm and judged on that partner's bout; Control is a duel won by holding the opponent still for ten seconds, no injury needed. Every entrant finishes with a placement. Sixteen per dorm qualify for the Academy Competition by role, whoever goes furthest in their bracket: Attack 4, Defense 3, Healing 3, Support 3, Control 3; winning is not required. Each bracket's champion earns 100 points and its runner-up 50.
2. Academy. Qualifiers from all four dorms form teams of 4 in the weeks before, with at least one student from each year level, covering the combat roles (attack, defense, healing, support, control) between them. Teams mix dorms and usually must, since no dorm covers every role well. Knockout bracket; the winning team represents the academy.
3. Kingdom. One team per academy, four in total, over three rounds in and around Avalon, the capital. Every competitor wears a ward seal; when it breaks they are pulled out for the rest of the round. Round one, outdoors in the Kingsmeadow beyond the city wall: from morning each team hunts for a Kingsbloom, and only three are planted; the judges take one per team at sunset, and the team without one goes out. Round two, in the Undercroft beneath the Royal Arena: ten rooms, each guarded by a bound beast; a team takes a room by defeating its beast and then being the only team inside for fifteen minutes, one member is enough, and the count stops while anyone from another team is in the room; a taken room can be taken again the same way. The two teams that hold four rooms at the same time go through. Round three, the final, on the arena sand: one team against the other. Great scrying crystals over the arena show the crowd the rounds fought away from the sand. Each member of the winning team earns 5,000 points, and the winning team is received by the King at Eldrasil Castle on the last evening. Only the teams travel: they arrive the day before and stay at the Whitestone Hotel beside the arena, and fly home the day after. Evenings are free in the city until 21:00, the hotel by 22:00. Halvard's team goes with Gavlan Haverton as its escort; the rest of the academy stays at school and follows the news.
4. World (abroad), Month 11 Week 3 Monday to Week 4 Monday. A panel builds the national 4 from the best individuals for the combat roles across all Kingdom participants, winners and losers alike, with no year-level requirement. Being picked off a losing team is a great honour. Six nations compete: four hold a standing invitation (Eldrasil, Yozakura, Norvaine and Caelmar), and two places go to an open qualification earlier in the year, which the Arslan Sultanate and Velmora have won in recent years ahead of Sunreach Bay and the rest. Yozakura, Norvaine and Caelmar are the usual favourites, and Yozakura won the competition before campaign Year 1. Eldrasil has never won in living memory. Every nation's four travels with a coach; Eldrasil's is Gavlan Haverton, as at the Kingdom.
Picked to fight together: in the competition before campaign Year 1, Gareth Valkaryn and Lucius Vortigern were the strongest in Eldrasil on paper; on the field Lucius's swarm ate Gareth's fire, and Yozakura took the title. Since then, at Gavlan Haverton's urging, the panel picks the four for how they fight together, not only for who is strongest.
Format: an opening banquet on arrival, where the Phase 1 groups are drawn; Phase 1 (below); Phase 2, two head-to-head matches on two days, the losers going out; a rest day; Phase 3, the Grand Final in the arena, the only straight team-against-team fight, then the closing banquet.
Phase 1 (two days, one group of three teams each day): Last Team Standing in a forest bounded by a barrier that shrinks all match long. Every competitor is teleported to a random spot, alone, and carries a crystal; when it breaks, that competitor is out. The first team to lose every member is out of the competition. The match runs until one team is left: it tops the group and is the top seed in Phase 2, and the other survivor goes through second.
Phase 2 (two days, one match a day): Siege, in an old fortress with two ward stones. The attacking team carries one Breach Rune, a siege working: planted on a ward stone with a minute of unbroken channelling, it fills for three minutes and then breaks the stone. A defender can unwind a planted rune with a minute of unbroken channelling of their own. Attackers win a round when the stone breaks or every defender is out; defenders win when they unwind the rune, when ten minutes pass without a planting, or when every attacker is out before one. Crystals work as in Phase 1 and are replaced each round; healing cannot mend a broken crystal. The first team to win four rounds goes to the Grand Final; the sides swap after the third round, and at three all a seventh round decides. The top seed chooses its first side and the side in a seventh round.
Prize: each member of the winning four earns 10,000 points and takes home a small replica of the World Trophy; the trophy itself stays with the winning nation until the next competition. Yozakura held it when campaign Year 1 began.
