Etiquette Class
Purpose: Turn powerful children into civilized mages. Magic gives a student the power to hurt, coerce, or take, and this is the one class that asks what they owe the people around them. Covers ethics, personal conduct, and enough psychology to recognise pressure, manipulation, and one's own worst impulses. The academy's quiet answer to the question of why graduates become healers and guards rather than criminals, dark mages, or cultists.
Activity: Moral dilemmas argued aloud, with students made to defend the side they disagree with. Case studies of mages who crossed the line, and how each of them started. Restraint drills: winning an argument without magic, refusing a request, de-escalating a fight. Lessons on consent, duty of care, and helping without being asked. Applied conduct: correspondence, titles and address, dining, and mock receptions, since graduates serve noble houses and Graduation is attended by nobles.
Marked on judgement and conduct, not politeness. A student can be flawless at a reception and still fail.
Covers: personal ethics, the legal line each type sits near (see Dark Magic Defense), the Noble Houses' Liaison, and conduct at Graduation.
