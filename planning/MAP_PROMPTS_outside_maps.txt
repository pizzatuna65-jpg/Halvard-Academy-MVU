Use the attached map of Halvard Academy as the strict reference for art style: copy its rendering, line work, colour palette,
lighting, level of detail and camera angle exactly, so that the two maps look like they were painted by the same artist for the
same atlas. That style is:
- a high bird's-eye view from the south, looking north at about 45 degrees, almost isometric, with only slight perspective;
- a bright, cheerful, hand-painted miniature look, like a storybook or cosy fantasy game world map: clean, crisp shapes with thin
  soft outlines, gentle cel-like shading, soft light from the upper left, small soft shadows, high-key and low contrast, nothing
  gritty, murky or photographic;
- small, simple, stylised trees repeated evenly: round puffy broadleaf trees in fresh yellow-green and lime, neat cone-shaped
  conifers in deep teal-green, and spring blossom trees in pastel pink and white dotted among them; light grassy ground shows
  between the trees in many places;
- a saturated but soft palette: fresh yellow-greens, teal-blue pines, pastel pink blossom, cream and pale grey stone, clear
  light-blue water;
- soft white clouds of mist drifting in from the outer edges and corners of the image, framing it;
- every feature drawn small and tidy, like a model on a table, so the whole map reads clearly at a glance.
Even the dark and sinister places (Blightbriar, the Gloaming, the cave) stay in this bright, soft, storybook style: they are
darker and stranger than the rest, never realistic or horror-like.

Make a 1536x1024 landscape map of the southern rim of a vast wild fantasy forest called the Grand Vast Forest, in early spring
(fresh pale greens, some bare branches, a little morning mist in the low places).

Keep it calm and readable, and make it one continuous landscape, not separate pictures placed on a forest background. Most of the
image is plain forest canopy in soft, simple shapes, with no extra features, ruins, paths or props. Seven places stand out from it,
well separated by wide stretches of forest, each with its own look so it stays recognisable when cropped and zoomed.

Every place must grow out of the land around it, with no hard outlines, no circles or patches, and no visible seams:
- The ground has one believable shape: low and flat in the south, rising gently to the north, with a rocky ridge across the
  north-west and a river valley down the east side. Cliffs, the falls and the terraces are parts of that terrain, not objects.
- Each place fades into the forest over a wide margin: its colours, trees and textures thin out gradually, the ordinary trees mix in,
  and the canopy closes over its edges in places. Light, shadow, haze and colour temperature are the same across the whole map.
- Tree species, sizes and colours change gradually across the map (smaller pines and birches near Base Camp, broad old trees towards
  the north), so the special places look like the far end of a gradient, not something different dropped in.

1. Base Camp (bottom centre, about 50% across, 86% down): a small natural meadow at the forest's southern edge, its grass worn
   and trampled, trees leaning in around it; three wooden airships with balloon envelopes floating at anchor above it, a ring of
   canvas tents round a campfire, one timber lodge half under the trees.
2. Blightbriar (middle left, about 20% across, 55% down): the forest slowly sickening: towards its centre the ordinary trees grow
   twisted and bare, thorn thickets creep up their trunks, and fungus shelves and tall mushroom towers in sour orange and violet take
   over, under a faint greenish haze. At its edges healthy and sick trees stand side by side.
3. The Gloaming (centre, about 45% across, 45% down): the trees grow older and bigger towards the centre of the map until they
   are enormous, and the canopy darkens to a dense black-green, with a few tiny points of pale blue light beneath. No border: the
   forest simply becomes ancient.
4. Silverstair Falls (middle right, about 75% across, 50% down): where the river valley steps down, the river drops over a wide
   mossy rock face in broad tiers of white water, trees growing on every ledge and spray softening the forest around it.
5. The Jade Terraces (upper right, about 82% across, 25% down, above the falls): the river valley above the falls is lined with
   stepped natural pools rimmed in pale gold stone, like Huanglong valley, in turquoise and jade green, with trees growing between
   and around the pools and the forest closing in on both sides of the valley.
6. The Grand Cave Network (upper left, about 15% across, 20% down): part of the north-west ridge, where the ground rises into a
   grey cliff with trees on its top and ledges, and one huge dark cave mouth opens at its foot, half hidden by the forest in front.
7. The Old Heart (top centre, about 50% across, 14% down): one colossal tree, by far the largest thing on the map, its crown
   rising high above the canopy and its roots disappearing into the surrounding forest; the trees around it grow larger as they
   near it.

A single river (the Silverrun) enters at the top right, runs through the Jade Terraces, drops over Silverstair Falls, then winds
south-east and leaves the image at the bottom right. The forest continues past every edge of the image and fades into haze along
the top edge.

Do not include any text, labels, place names, legend, compass rose, frame, border, scale bar or UI elements. No people or creatures.
